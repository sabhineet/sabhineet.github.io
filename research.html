Decoding Disk-Corona-Jet Connections of Stellar Mass Black Holes through Timing and Spectral Analysis

Supervisor Dr. Arka Chatterjee Assistant Professor, UPES Dehradun
Co-Supervisor Dr. Sudip K. Garain IISER Kolkata

Overview

Stellar-mass black holes in X-ray binary systems are nature's laboratories for studying extreme gravity, accretion, and relativistic jets. This thesis probes the physical coupling between the three key structural components: the accretion disk, the X-ray emitting corona, and the relativistic jet.

Methodology

  • Analysing archival and new X-ray data from the NuSTAR mission
  • Extracting and analysing light curves for variability characterisation
  • Computing time lag spectra between soft and hard X-ray bands
  • Constructing Power Density Spectra (PDS) to study quasi-periodic oscillations
  • Connecting timing signatures to physical disk-corona-jet geometry

Goals

To establish observational constraints on how energy and matter flow between the disk, corona, and jet in stellar-mass black hole binaries, potentially resolving long-standing questions about jet launching mechanisms.

External Project Aug 2025 – Present University of Delhi
Active

Stochastic Dynamics of Brownian Motion

Collaborator Dr. Raju Roychowdhury University of Delhi, Delhi

Overview

This project investigates the dynamics of Brownian motion in classical non-conservative systems — situations where energy is not conserved due to dissipative forces or external driving. Understanding these stochastic dynamics has broad applications in biophysics, statistical mechanics, and colloidal science.

Methodology

  • Deriving analytical solutions using Green's functions
  • Computing correlation functions and mean-square displacement analytically
  • Implementing Monte Carlo simulations for stochastic modeling
  • Statistical analysis of particle trajectories and ensemble averages

Tools & Methods

Green's Functions Monte Carlo Python Statistical Mechanics Stochastic Processes

Summer Internship Jun – Jul 2024 VECC, Kolkata
Completed

Development of Carbon and Carbon Monoxide Ion Beams for Si Surface Modification and Nanostructuring

Supervisor Dr. Prasanta Karmakar Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC), Kolkata

Overview

At the Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre — a premier nuclear research facility in India — this internship focused on developing and characterizing low-energy ion beams for precise surface modification of silicon substrates, with applications in semiconductor nanostructuring and quantum computing devices.

Key Contributions

  • Developed and optimised low-energy ion beams (C⁺, N⁺, N₂⁺, CO⁺) using an ECR Ion Source
  • Performed mass separation with dipole magnets for beam purity
  • Carried out ion implantation on Si(100) substrates
  • Verified implantation profiles using SRIM simulations
  • Conducted theoretical study of quantum computing devices and ion beam control systems

B.Sc. Thesis Aug 2023 – May 2024 UPES, Dehradun
Completed

A Comprehensive Study on Detection Techniques and Trends in Characteristics of Exoplanets

Supervisor Dr. Rashi Jain Research Fellow, University of Southampton, UK

Overview

A data-driven investigation into the statistical properties of exoplanets in the NASA archive, exploring how different detection techniques (transit photometry, radial velocity, direct imaging, microlensing) introduce systematic biases into the observed exoplanet population.

Key Contributions

  • Performed statistical analysis and graphical visualisation of NASA exoplanet archive data using TOPCAT
  • Identified trends in exoplanetary properties (mass, radius, orbital period, eccentricity) across detection methods
  • Implemented a Random Forest algorithm in Python to predict target exoplanet properties from known variables
  • Produced a trained ML model that fills observational gaps in the archive caused by detection technique biases
